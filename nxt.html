nxt

A to-do app you talk to. Say what's on your mind, however rambling, and nxt turns it into tasks, then picks the one thing you should do next and tells you why.

RoleProduct definition and UX/UI design
PlatformiPhone, Apple Watch and Android
FocusVoice-first input, conversational onboarding, AI suggestions

Onboarding, creating tasks by voice, and editing a task.

The problem

The overhead is the problem

Most to-do apps ask a lot before they give anything back. You type each task, pick a date, choose a list, set a reminder. For the people who most need help keeping track (busy parents, founders, anyone with an ADHD brain) that overhead is exactly what they don't have time for. Then they open the app and face fifty things at once.

So nxt does two things differently. You speak your tasks instead of typing them, and instead of a long list it suggests one next task, with a reason.

Voice first

One breath, two tasks

The test I kept coming back to was whether someone could say everything at once, the way they'd tell a friend. "Please set up a task that happens every week on Thursday evening to sort out the recycling bins, and then I need a task on Friday mornings to take the bins out before 7:30, otherwise they'll miss pick up." That should come back as two separate repeating tasks, with the right days and times, and no forms.

There's always a keyboard option too, for when talking out loud to your phone isn't an option.

Onboarding

Getting to know you, as a conversation

Suggesting the right next task needs context: when you get up, when you have energy, what matters to you. Rather than a settings form, onboarding asks a few light questions, then plays back what it's learned. I considered cutting that recap screen to make onboarding shorter, but kept it, because people are usually interested in seeing what the app has picked up about them.

Then it offers common tasks to start with, and you add your first one by voice. If you don't mention a time or how often, it asks a follow-up question, like a person would. It ends with confetti, because finishing things should feel good.

The onboarding conversation in Figma, with my notes for the developers under each screen.

The details

Fixing the edit screen

Voice gets a task in quickly, but people still need to tweak things. The first version split this across two separate forms, one to edit the task and one to edit how it repeats, which felt heavy on a phone.

I redesigned it as a single drawer. The title and notes are borderless, so you just tap and type. Due date, snooze and repeat all start empty, and each opens its own small screen with a scroller. Snooze will get quick picks like "this evening" or "next week" in a later version. When you save a repeating task, it asks whether the change is for this one or all future ones.
