nxt

A to-do app you talk to. Say what's on your mind, however rambling, and nxt turns it into tasks, then picks the one thing you should do next and tells you why.

RoleProduct designer and product lead
PlatformiPhone, Apple Watch and Android
FocusVoice-first input, conversational onboarding, AI suggestions

Speaking a to-do list, the task list it builds, the one suggested next task, and a reminder.

The problem

The overhead is the problem

Most to-do apps ask a lot before they give anything back. You type each task, pick a date, choose a list, set a reminder. For the people who most need help keeping track (busy parents, founders, anyone with an ADHD brain) that overhead is exactly what they don't have time for. Then they open the app and face fifty things at once.

So nxt does two things differently. You speak your tasks instead of typing them, and instead of a long list it suggests one next task, with a reason.

Voice first

One breath, two tasks

The idea was that you could brain-dump a complicated mental list out loud and nxt would sort it out. The AI picks out each task, sets it up sensibly using the context it already holds about you, and prioritises it alongside everything else.

A real example: "Please set up a task that happens every week on Thursday evening to sort out the recycling bins, and then I need a task on Friday mornings to take the bins out before 7:30, otherwise they'll miss pick up." That comes back as two separate repeating tasks, with the right days and times, and no forms to fill in.

There's always a keyboard option too, for when talking out loud to your phone isn't an option.

The recycling-bins request in the app, next to the App Store listing.

Onboarding

Getting to know you, as a conversation

Suggesting the right next task needs context: when you get up, when you have energy, what matters to you. Rather than a settings form, onboarding asks a few light questions, then plays back what it's learned. I considered cutting that recap screen to make onboarding shorter, but kept it, because people are usually interested in seeing what the app has picked up about them.

Then it offers common tasks to start with, and you add your first one by voice. If you don't mention a time or how often, it asks a follow-up question, like a person would. It ends with a confetti animation.
