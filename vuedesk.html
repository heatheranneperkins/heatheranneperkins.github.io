vuedesk

A self-serve desk, meeting-room and parking booking tool for hybrid offices. One price, $1 per space per month, with every feature included.

RoleMarket research, product definition, UX/UI and the final polish pass
TeamMe, a senior developer and a QA
TimelineFirst version live in about two weeks; second release a month later

Booking a desk, planning your week on mobile, a Slack check-in reminder, and the reporting cards. All screenshots use demonstration data.

Why we built it

Vantage was pay-as-you-go

Customers came to Vantage when they needed a study, which is great, but it's not income you can plan around. The business wanted a subscription product in the same world, aimed at the facilities teams we already knew.

Hybrid working has made desk booking normal, so I looked at what was out there. The tools ranged from heavyweight enterprise systems to add-ons for badge and sensor hardware. Even the ones that called themselves self-serve still needed a call to get set up, and kept features locked behind more expensive tiers.

So we went the other way. Web-based, nothing to install, sign up and go. One flat price per space, and you get everything. And because the people doing the booking are employees with phones in their hands, not facilities managers at a desk, it was designed mobile-first.

The pricing was the proposition, so it went in the headline.

The first two weeks

Desks first, everything else later

The first release was desk booking and nothing else. Meeting rooms were meant to be in it too, but they weren't ready in time, so I switched them off rather than ship them half-finished.

Two weeks was only possible because we didn't start from zero. We already had sign-up and subscription flows from other products, and the floorplan and space logic from Vantage. I wrote the stories and a style guide the AI coding tools could follow, our senior developer and QA built it, and I did the final polish pass while making the screenshots for the website.

About a month later the second release added meeting rooms, parking, hybrid working policies, Slack, and fuller reporting.

Admin

Keeping the settings out of the way

Admins need to manage spaces, people, departments and office policies. Someone booking a desk for Tuesday needs none of that, so they never see it. All the configuration lives in its own admin area, and the booking screens stay focused on finding a space.

The admin side is also where the most useful feedback came from. In a demo with one of our largest Vantage customers, they told us a single admin level wouldn't work for them. They needed people who could manage one region, or one building, or only view. I specced that up as the next piece of work.

Reporting

Showing how the office is really used

Building reports is familiar ground after years of Vantage dashboards. Workplace managers get attendance, desk, meeting-room and parking use at a glance, and can filter it by building or department.

The overview dashboard for workplace managers.

Where it got to

Our marketing team was a long way behind development, so outreach for vuedesk was only just starting. Even so, a handful of companies found the website and signed up on their own, and demos with some of our biggest Vantage customers went well.

If I did it again, I'd decide upfront how we'd measure whether it was working. We moved fast enough to ship two releases in six weeks, but we hadn't yet got to the stage of checking our assumptions against how people were actually using it.
