Vantage Space

Workplace occupancy studies without sensors. Observers walk a building with a phone or tablet and record how each space is being used, and the people who commissioned the study get the results back as dashboards, floorplans and raw data.

RoleHead of Product, 2018 onwards. I owned the backlog and led the product and UX/UI design
PlatformWeb app for setup and analytics; mobile and tablet app for observers
ScaleUsed by over 40% of the Fortune 100. 5.6M+ workspaces observed in 70+ countries

Study setup and the occupancy overview for study owners, with the observer app's onboarding. All screenshots use demonstration data.

The product

Good data, with almost no training

Vantage's customers are the people running occupancy studies: workplace consultancies and in-house real estate teams. They need data they can trust, often collected by observers who have never used the app before.

So the observer app had to be quick to learn and quick to use. A study manager could give someone a login and have them collecting reliable data with almost no training. Most of the design decisions in the app come back to that.

The observer app: tap a workspace on the floorplan, record what's there, move on. Each observation goes straight into the study overview.

The biggest change

More detail for CBRE, no extra work for everyone else

CBRE, one of our largest customers, needed to record an extra piece of information for a study they were running for a client. They wanted it badly enough that they'd already run one study by typing it into the notes field and sorting it out by hand afterwards.

It was useful data for any study, but it meant an extra step for observers. Our customers relied on being able to put someone new on the app and get good data straight away, so anything that made observing slower or harder to learn had a real cost. I needed to add it without making the app worse for everyone else.

I redesigned the observation flow so the extra detail was optional and switched on per study. Because this meant a big change to the data underneath, I also used it to fold in smaller speed improvements that support had been hearing about from other customers. We ran it as opt-in for about four months, turning it on study by study for our larger clients and walking them through it, then switched it on for everyone.

CBRE used it on their very next study, and it put them in a position to recommend Vantage across their global teams. Most other customers didn't notice the change, which was the aim. It was a big change underneath, nothing broke, and data collection got faster.

Reporting

Answering the question behind the request

One customer asked for a capacity filter. They could already get their answer by filtering room by room, but only if their naming was consistent or they knew the size of every room. When I talked it through with them, it turned out they didn't want a filter. They wanted to see how use changed with room size. So I designed a chart that shows the breakdown by capacity directly, and because that's a useful view for anyone, it went into the product for every customer.

Meeting-room supply against demand, and occupancy by room capacity.

The study comparison view also started as a customer request. It was about a week of development time, and I had to argue for it over more work on our internal tools. My case was that customers had asked for it, every customer could use it, and it gave marketing a good reason to get back in touch with our biggest clients. The CEO agreed, and it went out.

Occupancy by department and by workspace type, what people were doing through the day, and occupancy per tour across two studies.

Two ways of using the results

Facilities managers studying their own buildings mostly used the dashboards. Vantage didn't try to act as a consultant and explain what was happening, but it gave them the same charts the consultants used, with the patterns pulled out and room to dig into the detail.

Workplace consultants usually had their own report formats, or wanted to combine our data with other sources, so for them the important part was getting the data out. I scoped Vantage's API to give access to both the raw observations and the same views customers see in the dashboards, and later scoped an MCP server so an AI assistant could set up and manage a live study.
