Vantage Space

Designing an end-to-end platform for collecting workplace observations and making sense of the results.

RoleLead product design, UX/UI and product definition
PlatformWeb analytics and administration; mobile/tablet observation app
FocusComplex workflows, data visualisation, enterprise usability

Workplace data presented through dashboards and visual reporting. Screenshots use demonstration data.

The context

One platform. Different needs.

Vantage Space supports workplace occupancy studies. People carrying out observations need to record what they see quickly and accurately, often while moving around a building. The teams commissioning studies need to configure the work, monitor progress and interpret the data afterwards.

I led the UX, interface and interaction design across the experience, working closely with engineering, customers and commercial stakeholders. The recurring challenge was to make a data-rich product understandable without stripping away the detail that professional users depend on.

From observation to insight

The product spans study setup, observation workflows, progress tracking, dashboards and exports. Rather than treating each screen in isolation, I designed around the connections between these activities — how information is entered, structured, checked and eventually used.

Study management: making the setup and operational state of a study visible.

Connecting observations to a physical floorplan.

Giving teams visibility into collection progress.

Designing for different levels of detail

A major customer needed observers to capture more detailed occupancy information. Simply adding extra steps to the standard workflow would have slowed down the routine observations used by other customers.

I redesigned the interaction so richer information could be captured when needed, while keeping the everyday path efficient. The solution maintained compatibility with existing studies and supported a phased transition. It was adopted by the requesting customer and subsequently rolled out more broadly.

Design principle

More capability shouldn't automatically mean more friction. Advanced requirements can be accommodated without forcing every user through the same complexity.

Making the results legible

Different users need different views of the same underlying observations: high-level occupancy trends, comparisons, detailed breakdowns and spatial context. The reporting experience uses multiple visual forms so people can move between an overview and the underlying detail.

Comparison reporting: exploring differences between datasets.

Compact visual summaries for identifying patterns.

Supporting the next step: getting data out for further use.

What this work demonstrates

Designing for enterprise software means balancing competing requirements, maintaining clarity as features grow and understanding how small interaction choices affect real work. Vantage gave me sustained ownership of that challenge across connected web and mobile experiences.
