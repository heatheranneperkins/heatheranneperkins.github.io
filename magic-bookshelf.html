Magic Bookshelf

Personalised bedtime stories. Children design a cartoon version of themselves and their family, pick where the story happens, and then star in the words and the illustrations.

RoleProduct designer and product lead
PlatformiPhone and iPad
FocusAI for children, character design, pricing

The bookshelf, choosing where the story is set, and a page from a finished story.

Where it came from

Bedtime stories with my daughter as the hero

This one started at home. Like a lot of parents, I was playing around with ChatGPT and asked it for a bedtime story featuring my daughter and some kittens. She loved it, and I thought it would be a lovely thing to be able to do with her properly.

The big decision

Choices, not conversation

In this app the AI is the whole point: it writes the story and paints the pictures. But the audience is young children, and we were never going to build a way for a child to have an open conversation with an AI. So apart from giving a character a name, there's no typing at all.

Everything is done by tapping pictures. You build characters from a set of faces, hairstyles and clothes. You pick from illustrated settings like a pirate ship, outer space or a magic school. You choose between a story and a poem. The AI stays behind the scenes, and the only thing a child has to do is make visual choices. We even added a secret sparkle drawing game on the loading screen to entertain them while the story was being created!

Designing for what was coming

Characters before the AI could draw them

When we started, the illustrations were scenery only. Getting AI to put the same character into a picture was inconsistent at the time, and occasionally nightmare-inducing, which is not what you want at bedtime.

We designed for where image generation was going anyway. Children designed their characters visually from the start, so when the image models caught up, they could suddenly see themselves in their stories, down to the colour of their socks.

The character creator in Figma, and the same steps in the finished app: skin, hairstyle, eyes.

Growing it

What we added after launch

Once the core stories were working, we kept building on them:

Choose-your-own-adventure stories. At key points the story stops and the child picks what happens next.

Narration. Stories are read aloud, so children who can't read yet can listen along.

Other languages. Stories can be created in languages other than English.

Sharing. Families can send a finished book to someone else.

Pricing

From unlimited to credits

We launched with an unlimited monthly subscription. Image generation costs made that commercially unworkable, so we replaced it with a credit model.
