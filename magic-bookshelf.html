Magic Bookshelf

Personalised bedtime stories. Children design a cartoon version of themselves and their family, pick where the story happens, and then star in the words and the illustrations.

RoleProduct definition and UX/UI design
PlatformiPhone and iPad
FocusAI for children, character creation, read-aloud

The bookshelf, choosing where the story is set, and a page from a finished story.

Where it came from

Bedtime stories with my daughter as the hero

This one started at home. I was playing with an AI model and asked it for a story with my daughter in it. She loved it, and I found myself doing it again and again. That felt like a product.

The big decision

Choices, not conversation

In this app the AI is the whole point: it writes the story and paints the pictures. But the audience is young children, and we were never going to build a way for a child to have an open conversation with an AI. So apart from giving a character a name, there's no typing at all.

Everything is done by tapping pictures. You build characters from a set of faces, hairstyles and clothes. You pick from illustrated settings like a pirate ship, outer space or a magic school. You choose between a story, a poem or a choose-your-own-adventure. The AI stays behind the scenes, and the only thing a child has to do is make choices. While a story is being written, the loading screen is honest that the pictures won't always match the words exactly.

The character creator in Figma, and the same steps in the finished app: skin, hairstyle, eyes.

Reading together

Designing ahead of the technology

Children this age often can't read the story themselves, so it reads aloud. I designed narration that highlights each word as it's spoken. When it's paused, you can tap a word to hear it again, or hold to hear the whole sentence, with a little bounce and a tap of haptic feedback. I tried highlighting with text colour and with a background colour to see which was easier to follow.

I also noted on the board that word-by-word timing might not be technically possible yet while keeping the voice natural across a whole sentence. I'd rather design the version we want and know where the limits are than design down to them from the start.

Narration v3 in Figma: two ways of highlighting words as they're read.

Iterating

Version 2.1

Once the app was live, I went back over the screens that weren't working as well as they could. Shared books got a full-screen moment: an animated gift you tap to open, with a sound and a little shake. Book details became a full screen with the "Read now" button always pinned to the bottom. Sign-up moved to full screen too. I mocked up each change in light and dark against the current version, so we could compare them side by side.
